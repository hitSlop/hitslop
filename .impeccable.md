# hitSlop design context

## Audience

Everyday productivity users who want a tiny, local app for a specific job without adopting a large system or learning a development workflow.

## Core use cases

- Start a new mini app from a small, growing template library.
- Reopen a recent `.slop` document quickly.
- Keep app source, local data, and appearance together in an inspectable package.
- Change a document's theme or vibe-code CSS without rebuilding its WebAssembly app.

## Personality and direction

hitSlop should feel like an editorial creative studio: warm, tactile, concise, and a little unexpected. The launcher is a document browser and showcase, not a dashboard or card marketplace. Use strong typography, asymmetry, quiet separators, and one restrained warm accent. Avoid generic rounded-card grids, glassmorphism, neon-on-dark palettes, and decorative technical styling.

The app follows macOS light and dark appearance with deliberately composed warm palettes in both modes. Native controls should remain recognizable, keyboard-accessible, and fast.
